Adding reviews to your site is easy. Putting them in the right place is the part that matters. Reviews at the bottom of a page nobody scrolls to do very little. Reviews next to a button, at the moment someone is unsure, do a lot more.

Here are nine places to try, with the Reviewkit widget that fits each one. You do not need all nine. Start with two or three.

**Key takeaways**

- Put a small badge near every main button. Put full reviews on pages where people compare and think.
- Match the widget to the spot: badges for tight spaces, grid or carousel for open sections.
- Add reviews to one page at a time so you can see what changed.

## 1. Next to the main button in your hero section

The top of your homepage or landing page is where people decide whether to stay. A short line under your headline or button, such as stars and "Rated 4.7 from 1,200 reviews", gives instant proof.

**Use:** `[reviewkit_tp_micro_combo]` (stars and review count together).

## 2. In a bar at the top of the site

A thin bar or a header badge shows your score on every page without taking space from your content.

**Use:** `[reviewkit_tp_micro_trustscore]` or `[reviewkit_tp_micro_star]`. Keep it small so it does not push your content down. A badge that loads late and moves the page can annoy visitors. See [review widgets and site speed](/blog/review-widgets-site-speed-core-web-vitals).

## 3. Right above or below the sign-up or buy button

People hesitate just before they commit. A badge here answers "can I trust this company?" at the exact moment.

**Use:** `[reviewkit_tp_micro_button]` or `[reviewkit_tp_micro_combo]`.

## 4. On your pricing page

Pricing pages are where people compare you with other options. This is the best place for full reviews, because visitors are reading carefully.

**Use:** `[reviewkit_review style="grid"]` under the pricing table, or a carousel if space is tight. Choose reviews that talk about value, support and results, not only "great product".

## 5. On product pages

For a store, put a badge near the price or the add-to-cart button, and a set of reviews further down. Remember that Trustpilot reviews are about your business, not about a single product. We cover this in our [WooCommerce guide](/blog/trustpilot-reviews-on-woocommerce).

**Use:** `[reviewkit_tp_micro_combo]` near the button and `[reviewkit_review style="carousel"]` lower on the page.

## 6. On the cart and checkout pages

Doubt about a new store shows up most at checkout. A small, calm trust badge near the payment area helps. Do not put a large carousel here. You do not want to distract people from paying.

**Use:** `[reviewkit_tp_mini]` or `[reviewkit_tp_micro_trustscore]`.

## 7. Next to contact, quote and booking forms

Service businesses and agencies lose people at the form. A badge and two or three recent reviews beside the form tell the visitor that real customers had a good result.

**Use:** `[reviewkit_tp_mini]` plus `[reviewkit_review style="list"]` with a few reviews.

## 8. On your About and Testimonials pages

People who visit the About page are checking if you are real. A full set of reviews fits well here, and it is the one place where a longer list works.

**Use:** `[reviewkit_review style="list"]` or `style="grid"`.

## 9. In the footer, on every page

A small footer badge is a steady reminder on every page. It also gives people a place to click through to your Trustpilot profile.

**Use:** `[reviewkit_tp_micro_star]`.

## Rules that keep it working

- **One badge and one set of reviews per page** is usually enough.
- **Check mobile first.** Most visitors are on a phone. The Reviewkit widgets are responsive, but always look at the real page.
- **Link to your Trustpilot profile** so visitors can verify what they see.
- **Do not hide the bad.** A good mix of ratings looks more real. See [using Trustpilot reviews the right way](/blog/use-trustpilot-reviews-honestly).

## How to tell if it worked

1. Pick one page and one goal, such as clicks on the sign-up button.
2. Write down the number for the last few weeks.
3. Add the widget.
4. Compare again after a few weeks of similar traffic.

Traffic changes from week to week, so do not judge after two days. If you run an A/B test tool, test one placement at a time.

## Examples by business type

The same widget does a different job depending on what you sell. Here is how to think about it.

### Online store
Shoppers worry about delivery, returns and whether the shop is real. Put a small badge near the add-to-cart button and on the checkout page, and a carousel lower on the product page. Our [WooCommerce guide](/blog/trustpilot-reviews-on-woocommerce) has the code.

### Software or SaaS
Buyers compare features and prices on several sites. Put a grid of reviews on the pricing page and a badge under the main sign-up button. Pick reviews that mention support and ease of use.

### Agency or freelancer
Clients are hiring a person, so trust is personal. Put reviews beside your contact form and on your services pages. A list layout with longer reviews works well because clients read them.

### Local service business
People often decide fast. Put a badge in the header and a few reviews at the top of your booking or quote page. Mention the review count in your headline if it is strong.

## What not to do

- **Do not cover the page.** A floating box that follows people everywhere can feel pushy.
- **Do not hide your call-to-action** behind a large review block.
- **Do not copy the same set of reviews on every page.** Use filters to show different reviews on different pages, within the rules in [using Trustpilot reviews the right way](/blog/use-trustpilot-reviews-honestly).
- **Do not leave old reviews up** because you forgot about the widget. Check your key pages every month.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many reviews should I show at once?
On most pages, three to six is enough. On a testimonials page you can show more.

### Should I use a grid or a carousel?
Use a grid when you have the room and want people to scan quickly. Use a carousel when space is tight. See [which widget to use](/blog/choose-the-right-reviewkit-widget).

### Will adding reviews slow down my pages?
Any extra element adds some weight. Keep widgets few and below the first screen where you can, and test your pages. We explain how in [review widgets and site speed](/blog/review-widgets-site-speed-core-web-vitals).
