Reviewkit is free, and for many sites the free version is all you need. Reviewkit Pro is for people who want to go further. This article explains what Pro adds, who gets the most from it, and who should save their money.

**Key takeaways**

- The free plugin already includes seven badge widgets, three review layouts (grid, list, carousel), filters and automatic sync.
- Pro adds two things: **Fetch More Reviews** and a **Widget Builder** for review widgets.
- If you only need a badge and a few recent reviews, stay on free. Upgrade when you hit a real limit.

## What you get for free

Before looking at Pro, here is what the free plugin does:

- Connect your Trustpilot profile with **no API key**
- **Seven business widgets**: Mini, Starter, and five Micro badges (TrustScore, Star, Review Count, Button and Combo)
- **Three review layouts**: grid, list and carousel
- **Filtering** by star rating or date
- **Automatic sync** on a schedule, plus a Sync Reviews button
- Options to purge cached data
- Responsive widgets that adapt to your theme
- Shortcodes that work in the block editor and page builders

See [which widget fits which job](/blog/choose-the-right-reviewkit-widget) for the full tour.

## What Pro adds

Reviewkit Pro added two features in version 1.2.0:

### Fetch More Reviews
The free plugin gives you a solid set of your latest Trustpilot reviews. With **Fetch More Reviews**, Pro lets you bring in more of them. This helps if you have a long review history and want a bigger pool to pick from, for example for a large grid or for filtering down to the reviews that match a certain page.

### Widget Builder for review widgets
The **Widget Builder** lets you build and adjust your review widgets with more control than the standard settings. If you want your reviews to look exactly like your brand, or you want to create different review widgets for different pages, this is the feature to look at.

For the full and current list of what each plan includes, see the [pricing page](/pricing) and the [documentation](/docs).

## Free or Pro? A simple way to decide

### Stay on free if:
- You want a rating badge and a handful of recent reviews.
- You have a small number of reviews.
- The standard styling options already match your site.
- You are still testing whether reviews help your pages.

### Think about Pro if:
- You have **hundreds of reviews** and want to use more of them.
- You want **different review widgets for different pages**, such as pricing, product and about pages.
- You care about a **custom look** that goes beyond the standard settings.
- You build sites for **clients**, and want one plugin that handles more design requests.

## Questions to ask before you upgrade

1. **Have I tried the free version on my real pages?** Test first. If it does what you need, you save money.
2. **What exactly is limiting me?** Name the limit: not enough reviews, or a design you cannot reach. Pro should fix a specific problem.
3. **Does the plan cover my sites?** Check the number of sites and the support period on the [pricing page](/pricing).
4. **Will I use it?** A feature you never open is not worth paying for.

## How to upgrade without risk

1. Install the free plugin and set it up using the [step-by-step guide](/blog/display-trustpilot-reviews-on-wordpress).
2. Run it for a few weeks on your most important pages. Note which pages work best.
3. Upgrade only when you know what you want from Pro.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need Pro to show my Trustpilot rating?
No. The badge widgets are free.

### Do I need Pro to show reviews in a carousel or grid?
No. Grid, list and carousel layouts are in the free plugin.

### Does Pro change how I add widgets?
You still use shortcodes. Pro adds more ways to build and tune review widgets.

### Is Reviewkit made by Trustpilot?
No. It is an independent plugin and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Trustpilot.

## Example: a store with a long review history

Imagine a store with 2,000 Trustpilot reviews. On the free plugin, the product page shows a badge with the real count and a carousel of the latest reviews. That already works. Now the owner wants three different review sections: one for delivery on the shipping page, one for product quality on product pages, and one for support on the contact page, each with its own look. That is the kind of job where Pro's extra reviews and the Widget Builder start to earn their cost.

## Example: an agency with many clients

An agency adds Trustpilot reviews to ten client sites. Most clients are fine with a badge and one layout. Two want a layout that follows their brand guide exactly. The agency uses the free plugin for eight sites and Pro for the two that need more control. Upgrading only where needed keeps costs sensible.

## What Pro does not do

It is fair to say what Pro is not:

- It does not send review invitations. Use Trustpilot's own tools to collect reviews.
- It does not create reviews or change them. It shows what is on your Trustpilot profile.
- It does not promise Google star ratings in search. See [using Trustpilot reviews the right way](/blog/use-trustpilot-reviews-honestly).
- It is not affiliated with Trustpilot.
