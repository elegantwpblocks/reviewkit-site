Showing good reviews on your website is normal. But there are rules and good habits about how to do it. Two questions come up again and again:

1. Will my Trustpilot reviews show stars next to my site in Google?
2. Can I show only my best reviews?

This guide answers both, with links to the sources. It is general information, not legal advice. If you run a large business or advertise in regulated areas, talk to a lawyer.

**Key takeaways**

- Reviews about your own business, placed on your own site, do not earn star ratings in Google search. A widget does not change that.
- Do not suggest that the reviews you show are all of your reviews if you have picked only the good ones.
- Show your overall rating and review count, and link to your full Trustpilot profile.

## Question 1: Will Trustpilot reviews give me Google stars?

Probably not, and no plugin can promise it.

In 2019, Google [announced](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2019/09/making-review-rich-results-more-helpful) that "self-serving" reviews would no longer get review stars for LocalBusiness and Organization pages. A review is self-serving when it is about your business and placed on your own site. Google said embedding a third-party widget is seen as controlling the process, so using a widget does not fix it.

What still works:

- **Product reviews** can still qualify for stars on product pages, through Product structured data. These are reviews of a specific product, not of your company.
- **Independent sites** that collect reviews about other businesses, such as a directory, can be eligible.

Google's current [review snippet guidelines](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/review-snippet) are the place to check the rules. In July 2026, Google also added a line saying not to include fake or undisclosed incentivized reviews on the page or in structured data, and that pages breaking the guidelines can face a manual action.

**What to do:** Do not add fake review markup to your own site. Use Reviewkit for what it is good at: showing trust to people who are already on your page. The value is in conversion, not in star ratings in search.

## Question 2: Can I show only my best reviews?

Choosing which reviews to feature is common, and showing a few positive reviews in a marketing section is generally fine. The problem starts when it misleads people.

The US Federal Trade Commission's rule on consumer reviews, finalized in 2024, bars a business from misrepresenting that the reviews on a review portion of its website are all or most of the reviews it received, if it has suppressed reviews because of their rating or negative tone. See the [FTC announcement](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials). Law firms that wrote about the rule note that featuring selected positive reviews elsewhere on a site can be acceptable as long as it does not give the impression that they represent all reviews. Other countries have their own consumer protection rules about misleading reviews.

The rule is about being honest with your visitors. These habits keep you on the safe side:

1. **Show the real overall number.** Display your actual TrustScore and total review count with a badge such as `[reviewkit_tp_micro_combo]`.
2. **Link to your full Trustpilot profile,** so anyone can see every review.
3. **Label filtered reviews.** If you filter, say so: "Recent 4 and 5 star reviews. Read all 1,240 reviews on Trustpilot."
4. **Do not delete or hide negative feedback** to make a public review section look better.
5. **Never write or buy reviews.** The FTC rule also bans fake reviews and buying reviews of a particular sentiment.

## How Reviewkit's filters fit in

Reviewkit lets you filter reviews by star rating or by date. Good uses:

- Show the **newest reviews**, so the page is current. This is a fair use and also what buyers want.
- Feature **4 and 5 star reviews** in a marketing section, with the overall rating and a link to all reviews nearby.

Not a good use: showing only 5-star reviews while your real rating is much lower, with no sign of the full picture.

A mix also looks better. Research from Northwestern's Spiegel Research Center found that purchase likelihood was highest at ratings in the 4s, and that a perfect 5.0 can look too good to be true. See [online review statistics for 2026](/blog/online-review-statistics-2026).

- [ ] My site shows my real overall rating and review count
- [ ] There is a link to my full Trustpilot profile

- [ ] I have not added review structured data for my own business
- [ ] I have not paid for, written or edited any reviews
- [ ] Reviews on the page are recent

## How to write an honest label

Short and clear labels work best. Here are examples:

- "Our latest reviews on Trustpilot" (shows the newest, no filtering)
- "Recent 4 and 5 star reviews. See all 1,240 reviews on Trustpilot." (filtered, and says so)
- "Rated 4.6 from 1,240 reviews on Trustpilot" (overall, with the real count)

Avoid wording that suggests the reviews are a complete sample if they are not, such as "Here is what all of our customers say".

## What about review markup?

Some people try to add review structured data to their own pages using reviews from a widget. Do not do this for your own business. Google treats those as self-serving, and Google's guidelines say pages that break its structured data rules can lose rich results or face a manual action. If you sell products and have real product reviews, mark those up with Product structured data, following Google's current guidelines, and validate it with Google's Rich Results Test.

## Different countries, different rules

The FTC rule applies in the United States. The United Kingdom, the European Union and other regions also have rules about misleading commercial practices and fake reviews. If you sell to customers in several countries, the safest approach is the honest one: real reviews, real totals and no tricks. Check the rules where you operate.

No. Reviewkit displays your Trustpilot reviews so visitors can see them. It is not a schema tool and does not promise Google star ratings.

You should not hide negative reviews to make a review section look better. If a review breaks Trustpilot's rules, report it to Trustpilot through their process.

Yes. Showing recent reviews is fair and useful, especially with your overall rating and a link to your profile.
